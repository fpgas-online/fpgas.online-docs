# What fpgas.online ran on the ps1 blades, 7 October 2026

**You look after the blades at ps1 and want to know what we ran on them on 7 October 2026, what it changed,
and what followed.** What each blade still needs is on [Acorns at ps1](ps1.md); every time here is Adelaide
time.

## Test 4: JTAG with the header's serial port off (pi20 at ps1)

For this test pi20 at ps1's link in the gateway's `/srv/tftp/` was pointed at a copy of the shared boot
directory, with two changes made in the copy only: in `config.txt`'s `[all]` section `enable_uart=1` became
`enable_uart=0` and the line `uart_2ndstage=1` was taken out; in `cmdline.txt` the word
`console=serial0,115200` was taken out ([verifying 3](../building/compute-blade/verifying-3.md) has the
steps, both ways of making them and their undo). The blade was rebooted into the copy at 14:22 and was up again at 14:25.

In that boot GPIO14 had no user, the login prompt went to the Compute Module 5's own debug serial port (not
on the header), so nothing more had to be taken off the header's pins, and the check's `jtag` test read the
FPGA's IDCODE and device DNA (after checking that GPIO14 followed the Pi's pull both ways, verifying 3's step
for a blade with no 470 Ω in its J2 wire, as pi20 at ps1 has). Nothing was loaded into the card in test 4. The IDCODE read
`0x13631093`, the same part as on 20 September 2026 under kernel 6.12.75, when it read `0x3631093` (that reading
left out the version digit); the DNA was `0x0028e5c45e304854`. Earlier on 7 October, with the serial port on,
the check's `jtag` test had failed: GPIO14 is held by the serial port.

## Test 6: converting pi20 at ps1's card, begun and stopped

**Do not load a design into a card on a blade or convert it: that is not in this guide yet, because of what
follows.**

In the same boot, at 15:23, a load of our SoC into the FPGA (its SRAM, not its flash) over JTAG worked. The
card's PCIe endpoint did not come back on a bus rescan. At step 1b a root-complex re-probe failed (the bind
answered "No such device" and the root port was gone too), the run stopped there, and the blade was rebooted
at 15:52.

**From then on pi20 at ps1 restarted every 2 to 3 minutes until about 18:09, then stayed up; one boot, from
about 17:06, stayed up until we rebooted it at 17:20:12. No power cycle was run by us; whether the load, the re-probe
or the reboot caused it is not known.** An `openFPGALoader --reset` at 17:19 put the vendor's sample image back
in the FPGA, from the flash, and at 17:20:12 we rebooted the blade into the shared boot files (the next section). **The Acorn's flash was never written**: at 18:10 the card read as before the
tests, `10ee:7011` at 5 GT/s x1.

## The gateway's boot files

At 17:19:48 pi20 at ps1's link was put back at the shared directory `/srv/nfs/rpi/trixie/boot`, and the copy
was removed. **`config.txt` and `cmdline.txt` in Carl's shared boot directory were never changed**: they
read the same before and after (the two files we checked; the changes were made in the copy only). Every boot of pi20 at ps1 since 17:20 has come from the shared
directory (the gateway's TFTP and NFS logs), and at 18:10 its header's serial port was on again (`serial0`
was `ttyAMA0`).

## The Compute Module 4 blades

On 20 September 2026 JTAG on pi14 at ps1 got no response (TCK floating: its P1 cable unmated or its TCK wire
open). On 7 October 2026 pi14 at ps1 and pi18 at ps1 had only logins, pin reads, a pull test on GPIO2, GPIO3
and GPIO4 (each pull set and put back) and `vcgencmd`, which hung for good on both. **No check and no JTAG
has been run on a Compute Module 4 blade since 20 September.**

## pi20 at ps1's missing resistor

pi20 at ps1's serial pair is direct on GPIO14 and GPIO15 without the resistor. From the check's code, not
from a run on a blade: `fpgas-verify`'s `p2-serial` test never has both ends driving a wire at once (the Pi's
pins are inputs while the FPGA drives, and the FPGA's are inputs while the Pi drives), so it does not rely
on the resistor, and the fpgas.online design treats J2 as an input except while that test drives it. What the missing resistor does not
survive is a design that drives J2, such as pin-ID.
